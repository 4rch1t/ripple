How this system works

1. Forecast. For every medicine at every facility, the system tracks recent consumption and projects forward to estimate days of stock remaining — always as a range, never a single number, because demand is noisy.
2. Detect the real signal. One facility running low is usually just noise. The system only raises an alert when several nearby facilities are running low on the same medicine at the same time — that correlated pattern is what suggests a real regional shortage forming, not a fluke.
3. Look for a fix nearby. When an alert fires, the system checks whether any facility within reach has spare stock of that medicine above its own safety buffer. If so, it proposes a specific transfer (from → to, how much, how far).
4. Escalate when there's no fix. If nobody nearby has spare stock (or the distance is too far to move medicine in time), the system flags that medicine for supplier escalation instead of pretending redistribution can solve it.
5. Rank everything. All alerts are combined into one prioritized list, scored by: how critical the medicine is (life-saving vs. general), how soon it runs out, how many people are served by the affected facilities, and whether a transfer is actually possible.

About this demo

The underlying data is a real supply-chain dataset reshaped into a network of facilities. One realistic regional shortage scenario was deliberately injected into two city clusters so the detection and redistribution logic have something concrete to catch — every number you see downstream of that (the forecast, the alert, the transfer, the ranking) is computed live by the system, not scripted.

How to read this dashboard

The map

Each dot is one facility (hospital, PHC, or pharmacy). Color = risk level:

  • Red / critical — this facility is part of an active regional alert, or has a medicine at 2 days of stock or less.
  • Yellow / warning — lowest medicine here has 7 days of stock or less.
  • Green / normal — nothing urgent right now.

Click any dot to select that facility across every panel below, and to open a quick-summary popup on the map itself.

Panel 1 — Needs Supplier Restock

Medicines where the shortfall can't be solved by moving stock from elsewhere in the network — every nearby facility is also short, or too far away to reach in time. These need a fresh delivery from the supplier, not a transfer.

Panel 2 — Ranked Action List

Each card is one alert, ordered by priority score (top-right, 0–1, higher = more urgent). A card tells you: which medicine, which facilities are affected, how many days of stock are left (with a likely range in parentheses), and what to do: transfer stock from a named facility, escalate to the supplier, or both.

Panel 3 — Facility Medicines

Every medicine stocked at the selected facility, soonest-to-run-out first. Click a row to plot it in panel 5.

Panel 4 — Transfers In / Out

Any proposed transfers involving the selected facility: incoming (it receives surplus from elsewhere) or outgoing (it's the surplus source for another facility).

Panel 5 — Stock Trend

The solid line is stock on hand, the dashed line is daily consumption, for the facility + medicine currently selected. A steady decline with no recovery is what a forming shortage looks like.

Selecting a facility

Click a map dot, a card in panel 2, or a row in panel 3 — whichever you click, all five panels update and highlight matching entries (a blue outline marks anything related to the selected facility in panels 1 and 2).
